##### 1. What is Access Control?

Access control is a set of rules that decides **who** can get into a system (like a network or file) and **what** they are allowed to do.

##### 2. The IAAA Process

The easiest way to understand how this works is through the four steps of **IAAA**:

- **Identification:** This is the first step where the system recognizes you. Every user needs a **unique ID** so the system can tell them apart from everyone else.
    - _Think of it as:_ Telling the system your username.
- **Authentication:** This is proving you are actually the person you claim to be. You do this using things like passwords or fingerprints.
    - _Simple Definition:_ **"Who are you?"**
- **Authorization:** Once you are "in," the system decides what you can actually touch. You might be allowed to read a file but not delete it.
    - _Simple Definition:_ **"What are you allowed to do?"**
- **Accounting:** This is the final step that tracks everything you do. It creates a log of your actions so the company can monitor usage or investigate if something goes wrong.
    - _Result:_ This provides **accountability**.

##### 3. The "Pentagon" Analogy

Imagine you work at the Pentagon:

- **Authentication** is the guard checking your ID at the front gate to let you in the building.
- **Authorization** is the rules that say you can go into the cafeteria, but you are _not_ allowed in the top-secret war room.

##### 4. What is AAA?

In the industry, people often group the last three steps together as **AAA**: **A**uthentication, **A**uthorization, and **A**ccounting.
